Every failure survived progressively stricter execution modeling, three
adversarial audits, and two defect fixes that *removed advantages* the
strategy had been enjoying. The composed answer:

**Mechanical ICT/SMC constructions — order blocks, FVGs, liquidity
sweeps, structure breaks, killzones, and their aggregated "narrative" —
carry no positive directional information on crypto or index futures at
intraday horizons. Where they carry any information at all, the sign is
inverted: the higher the mechanized SMC conviction, the *lower* the
forward return.**

## The one new hypothesis this program generated (not tested here)

The inversion is too consistent to leave unstated: ETH and ES both show
monotone conviction→return inversion. A candidate interpretation is that
these widely-taught patterns mark where retail entries concentrate and
get faded. **This is an observation formed on burned/in-sample data and
is NOT a validated fade strategy.** Testing it would be a new program
with its own preregistration, its own decision rule, and fresh (or
formally re-consecrated, never-touched) holdout data — noting up front
that most inverted cells are individually weak and the monotone shapes
may be drift artifacts of bull-heavy samples.

## What survives the program

The instrument outlived every hypothesis it tested: a lookahead-safe
detector stack with prefix-consistency proofs, a pessimistic 1m-resolved
execution engine hardened by three audits, matched nulls with
diagnostics, block bootstraps, funnel instrumentation, forward-return
regime diagnostics, a preregistration/stopping-rule workflow that spent
**zero** holdouts on dead hypotheses, and an interactive lookahead-honest
replay UI. Total external data spend: ~$31.

Research is concluded. Any future work in this repository is a new
program: new hypothesis, new preregistration, new data protocol.
